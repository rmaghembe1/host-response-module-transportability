# Financial-First Journal Screening Strategy

## Purpose

This document revises the journal-targeting strategy for the host–pathogen transcriptome transportability manuscript. The manuscript has no dedicated funding for article processing charges. Journal selection must therefore prioritize financial feasibility before scientific scope, prestige, indexing or speed.

## Core constraint

The author has no funding for APC payment for this manuscript. A journal should not be treated as a practical target unless one of the following is true:

1. The journal offers a standard no-APC publication route.
2. The journal is hybrid/subscription and allows non-open-access publication without APC.
3. The journal offers an automatic 100% APC waiver for the corresponding author's country/institution.
4. The journal provides a clearly confirmed institutional agreement or publisher arrangement covering the APC.
5. A full discretionary waiver can be requested before or during submission and the journal/publisher indicates that full waiver is realistic.

## Revised journal-screening hierarchy

### Gate 1 — Financial feasibility

Before assessing scientific fit, determine:

- Is the journal fully open access or hybrid/subscription?
- What is the APC?
- Is there a no-APC publication route?
- Is Tanzania eligible for an automatic full APC waiver?
- If not automatic, is a discretionary full waiver possible?
- Can waiver eligibility be confirmed before submission?
- Is there an institutional agreement, Research4Life-linked arrangement, EIFL/COTUL agreement or publisher partnership that covers APCs?

### Gate 2 — Scientific fit

Only journals passing Gate 1 should be assessed for:

- Host-response transcriptomics scope.
- Infectious disease relevance.
- Systems immunology or computational biology fit.
- Acceptance of public-data reanalysis.
- Acceptance of module/pathway interpretation without wet-lab validation.
- Openness to reproducibility and code availability.

### Gate 3 — Submission practicality

For journals passing Gates 1 and 2, check:

- Article type.
- Word limits.
- Abstract structure.
- Figure/table limits.
- Supplementary-file policy.
- Reference style.
- Data/code policy.
- Expected review time.
- Indexing and visibility.

## Revised journal risk categories

### Tier A — financially safest

Hybrid or subscription journals that allow standard publication without APC. These are preferred if scope fit is acceptable.

### Tier B — acceptable if verified

Fully open-access journals with automatic 100% APC waiver for the corresponding author's country or confirmed institutional/publisher coverage.

### Tier C — risky

Fully open-access journals with discretionary or case-by-case waiver only. These should not be the first target unless the journal confirms that a full waiver is realistic before submission.

### Tier D — avoid unless funding appears

Fully open-access journals with high APC and no reliable full-waiver route.

## Immediate implications for current candidate journals

### BMC Genomics

Scientific fit remains strong, but financial feasibility must be verified before it can remain the first target. Because BMC Genomics is fully open access, it is only suitable if the APC is fully waived or covered.

### BMC Medical Genomics

Scientific fit remains strong/moderate, but the same financial constraint applies. It is only suitable if a full waiver or coverage route is confirmed.

### Frontiers in Immunology

Scientific fit may be good, but Frontiers fee support is generally case-by-case and may include full or partial waivers. This makes it financially risky unless full fee support can be confirmed before submission.

### Scientific Reports

Scientific fit is possible, but it is fully open access and can carry a high APC. It should be considered financially risky unless a full waiver or coverage route is confirmed.

### Hybrid/subscription alternatives

Hybrid or subscription journals with no mandatory APC should now be actively searched. These may become preferred targets even if their scope fit is slightly less direct, provided they allow publication without open-access charges.

## Revised current recommendation

Do not commit to BMC Genomics, BMC Medical Genomics, Frontiers in Immunology or Scientific Reports until APC waiver or no-cost publication feasibility is verified. The next step should be a financial-first journal audit that searches for:

1. Hybrid/subscription journals in infection transcriptomics, host response, immunology, genomics and computational biology.
2. Fully open-access journals with automatic 100% waiver eligibility for Tanzania.
3. Publisher agreements relevant to Tanzania, including Research4Life, EIFL, COTUL and institutional arrangements.
4. Journals that allow waiver requests before submission.

## Interpretation boundary

Financial screening does not change the scientific framing of the manuscript. The manuscript remains a fixed-module transportability analysis of host-response programmes, not diagnostic classifier discovery, diagnostic model validation, gene rediscovery, module redefinition or causal validation.
