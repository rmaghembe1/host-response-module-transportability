# CMI Stepwise Manuscript Revision Plan

## Purpose

This document defines the final stepwise revision strategy for converting the current CMI-facing v0.8 manuscript into a polished submission-grade CMI Original Article.

## Governing CMI requirements

The revision should follow CMI Original Article expectations:

- Structured abstract with Objectives, Methods, Results and Conclusions.
- Abstract maximum 300 words.
- Main text preferably below 2500 words.
- Main text divided into Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion.
- Maximum 30 references.
- Vancouver numbered references with DOI where available.
- British spelling.
- Tables submitted as editable text.
- Figure captions supplied separately.
- Required transparency, authorship, funding, conflicts, data availability and generative AI statements.

## Revision steps

### Step 1 — Remove internal notes from manuscript body

Remove internal-facing notes that should not appear in the submitted manuscript, including:

- Publication route note.
- Standalone internal interpretation-boundary warning.
- Draft/package language.
- Statements about APC route inside the manuscript body.

Retain the scientific caution by integrating it naturally into Methods, Results, Discussion and limitations.

### Step 2 — Add explicit CMI IMRaD headings

Ensure the main text contains clear major headings:

- Introduction
- Methods
- Results
- Discussion

### Step 3 — Convert front matter and end matter to CMI style

Clean the title page, remove role labels from author lines, and move contributions/transparency statements to CMI-compatible end matter.

### Step 4 — Add Vancouver citations and references

Add a compact, relevant reference list with no more than 30 references, including CMI host-response articles, dataset sources, limma, Gene Ontology, Benjamini–Hochberg and key clinical/biological background.

### Step 5 — Add CMI generative AI declaration

Add the required CMI/Elsevier AI-assisted technologies declaration before the references.

### Step 6 — Convert to British spelling

Apply British/international scientific English consistently.

### Step 7 — Ensure Table 1 is editable and submission-ready

Embed Table 1 in editable text form or provide a clean standalone table file with clear manuscript linkage.

### Step 8 — Confirm figure package and legends

Keep figure files separate and retain figure legends in the manuscript.

### Step 9 — Re-check word count and CMI limits

Confirm abstract remains ≤300 words and main text remains preferably <2500 words.

### Step 10 — Final DOCX/package audit

Generate the final DOCX, audit headings, references, figure/table callouts, required declarations and submission files.

## Discussion-writing principle

The Discussion should interpret results in relation to the study objectives and biological meaning. It should compare findings with relevant literature and explain why results are similar to or different from prior host-response studies. It should not read as a defensive justification of the analysis. The central message should remain: fixed-module transportability analysis complements diagnostic classifier-centred infection transcriptomics but does not constitute diagnostic validation or causal inference.
