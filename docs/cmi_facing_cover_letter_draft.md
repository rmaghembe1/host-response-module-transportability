# CMI-Facing Cover Letter Draft

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “Site-aware discovery and external transportability of bacterial- and viral-associated host-response modules in public infection transcriptomes,” for publication in *Clinical Microbiology and Infection*.

This manuscript reports a reproducible host-response transcriptomic analysis of bacterial-versus-viral infection biology across public datasets. We used GSE211567 for site-aware discovery and conservative module locking, then tested the locked modules by fixed-module projection in the independent GSE73461 infection cohort. The study was designed deliberately as a biological module-transportability analysis rather than a diagnostic classifier study. No genes were reselected, no modules were redefined, and no diagnostic model was trained or validated in the external cohort.

The main finding is that all five locked discovery modules retained expected-direction concordance in GSE73461. The strongest external support was observed for antiviral/interferon-associated modules and for the bacterial mitochondrial respiration/OXPHOS module, whereas the bacterial cytoplasmic translation/ribosomal module remained directionally concordant but borderline. These results support fixed-module transportability as a conservative framework for studying host-response architecture across heterogeneous infection transcriptomic cohorts.

We believe the manuscript will be of interest to readers of *Clinical Microbiology and Infection* because it addresses a central infectious-disease problem: how to interpret host-response programmes associated with bacterial and viral infection across heterogeneous public cohorts while avoiding overstatement of diagnostic readiness. The work is relevant to infection biology, clinical microbiology research and antimicrobial-stewardship-oriented host-response studies, but it explicitly does not claim diagnostic model validation or clinical test readiness.

The analysis is fully reproducible from public datasets, with scripts, decision logs, audit outputs, module-scoring files, manuscript-facing tables and publication-grade figures organized in the project repository. The manuscript uses only publicly available de-identified transcriptomic data and did not involve new recruitment of human participants or new collection of biospecimens.

Because this work has no dedicated funding for article processing charges, we intend to submit through the standard subscription/non-open-access publication route rather than optional open access, unless full open-access coverage is confirmed.

All authors have approved the manuscript for submission. We confirm that the manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere and has not been published previously.
