# CMI versus Journal of Microbiological Methods Target-Fit Decision

## Purpose

This document compares the two strongest financial-first journal targets after live verification of hybrid/subscription no-APC publication routes: Clinical Microbiology and Infection (CMI) and Journal of Microbiological Methods (JMM).

## Shared financial advantage

Both journals are financially attractive because they have a subscription/non-open-access publication route with no publication fee charged to authors. This satisfies the manuscript's core financial constraint: no APC funding is available.

## Option 1 — Clinical Microbiology and Infection

### Strengths

- Stronger prestige and visibility in infectious diseases and clinical microbiology.
- No-APC subscription route appears financially feasible.
- Good fit for infection biology if the host-response module transportability is framed as relevant to infectious-disease interpretation and antimicrobial-stewardship biology.
- More suitable than a methods journal if the manuscript remains primarily biological and translational.

### Weaknesses

- Likely high selectivity.
- May expect stronger clinical microbiology, infectious-disease or clinical relevance than the current manuscript provides.
- Current manuscript does not develop a clinical diagnostic tool, prospective cohort or direct clinical implementation evidence.
- May require tightening the title, abstract and discussion toward infection-relevant host-response biology.

### Required adaptation

- Strengthen the clinical microbiology relevance without overclaiming diagnostic validation.
- Emphasize public infection transcriptomes, bacterial-versus-viral host-response biology and cross-cohort transportability.
- Keep clear that the manuscript is not a diagnostic classifier or clinical test.
- Consider adding antimicrobial-stewardship relevance only as background motivation, not as a validated application.

### Risk level

Moderate/high scientific selectivity risk, but low financial risk if subscription route is used.

## Option 2 — Journal of Microbiological Methods

### Strengths

- No-APC subscription route appears financially feasible.
- More tolerant of methodological workflow framing.
- The manuscript's reproducible pipeline, decision logs, module locking, fixed-module projection, QC audits and figure export standards can be framed as a transparent computational workflow.
- Potentially lower selectivity barrier than CMI.

### Weaknesses

- The journal is methods-focused, while the current v0.4 manuscript is mainly biological and transcriptomic rather than a new software/tool/method paper.
- The manuscript would need substantial reframing toward methodological contribution.
- Biological findings may be treated as an application example rather than the main contribution.
- The current title and discussion would need revision to emphasize method/workflow utility.

### Required adaptation

- Reframe as a reproducible computational workflow for fixed-module transportability in infection transcriptomics.
- Make the discovery/projection firewall, audit trail and module-scoring framework central.
- Present GSE211567 and GSE73461 as a worked example of the workflow.
- Potentially add a workflow figure or supplementary reproducibility schematic.

### Risk level

Moderate scientific-fit risk due to methods framing, but low financial risk if subscription route is used.

## Comparative decision

### If prioritizing best scientific prestige and infection readership

Prefer **Clinical Microbiology and Infection**, accepting higher selectivity risk.

### If prioritizing safer methodological fit and reproducibility framing

Prefer **Journal of Microbiological Methods**, but revise the manuscript to make the method/workflow contribution more central.

### Current recommendation

The best first target is **Clinical Microbiology and Infection**, provided the manuscript is polished to strengthen infection/translational relevance while preserving the non-diagnostic and non-causal interpretation boundaries.

The best fallback target is **Journal of Microbiological Methods**, especially if CMI is judged too selective or if the manuscript is reframed as a reproducible computational workflow paper.

## Recommended target order

1. Clinical Microbiology and Infection
2. Journal of Microbiological Methods
3. Diagnostic Microbiology and Infectious Disease
4. Immunology
5. Genes and Immunity
